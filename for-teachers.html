What this site does and doesn't do

Every game here runs entirely in the browser. Nothing about your child is saved — no scores, no names, no progress, no cookies, no accounts. Close the tab and it is gone. That is deliberate, and it has one consequence worth knowing: a child cannot pick up where they left off. Where a game has difficulty levels, they are chosen rather than earned, so you can put a child straight back where they were.

You can also add the games to a phone or tablet home screen, and they will work with no internet at all — on a plane, in a car, anywhere. Installing saves a copy of the games themselves onto the device; it still saves nothing about your child.

There are no ads and no third-party scripts of any kind, so nothing follows your child around the internet afterwards. Games work offline once loaded and need no installation.

Some games teach a specific skill. A few are simply fun — the table says which is which, honestly.

Every chosen net folds up in 3D, so a wrong answer shows its doubled-up faces or open side instead of just a cross.

🧩 Logic & Puzzles

GameAgeTimeWhat it practises
Memory Match 3+ ~5m Visual memory and concentration — holding a picture and its place in mind while looking elsewhere.
Peekaboo Pond 3+ ~5m Watching and remembering: following a hidden friend as the flowers slide around, then finding it. It gets trickier only as the child gets it right, and a wrong flower just holds a surprise.

Pebble Push 5+ ~10m Planning ahead: pebbles can only be pushed, never pulled, so a careless push can wedge one in a corner. 18 levels from one pebble to three, every one checked solvable by computer, with undo, restart and a hint that knows the way.

Frozen Pond 7+ ~10m Planning several moves ahead and working backwards from the goal: on ice you slide until a rock stops you, so the route to the fish has to be thought through. 18 levels, each with its fewest-slides target, all checked solvable by computer, with undo, restart and hints.
Missing Tile 7+ ~10m Matrix reasoning: spotting what changes along the rows and down the columns of a 3×3 grid, then finding the one tile that fits the gap. 36 puzzles from one rule to three, each checked by computer to have exactly one answer, with hints that name the rule and a wrong tile that explains itself. Spot the Difference 7+ ~10m Careful, systematic looking and visual memory: five to eight differences between two busy pictures, from a missing item or a far colour to a changed shade, an extra apple, a flipped boat or a tilted tree. Free hints, no clock, stars for finding them all.
2048 Slider 14+ ~10m Doubling and powers of two, plus planning several moves ahead under a shrinking board.
Minesweeper 14+ ~10m Deduction from limited information — proving where a mine must be rather than guessing.
Sudoku 14+ ~20m Constraint reasoning and elimination. Every puzzle has exactly one solution, so correct logic is never punished.
Word Guess 14+ ~5m Vocabulary and deduction — narrowing thousands of five, six and seven-letter words down with each guess.
Laser Mirrors 14+ ~15m The law of reflection (angle in = angle out) and the spatial reasoning to follow a beam through several bounces: turn mirrors and splitters so laser beams light every crystal at once, with double crystals and red, blue and purple light in the later groups. 24 levels, each checked by computer to have one answer, with undo, reset and solver hints.
Twist Cube 14+ ~10m Reading a notation (U, D, L, R, F, B, and ′ for anticlockwise) and working backwards: a scramble of up to 10 twists is undone by reversing the order and flipping each twist. It also builds 3D spatial reasoning, keeping track of the faces as the whole cube turns.
Log Jam 14+ ~15m Planning several moves ahead and reasoning backwards from the goal: which log has to move before that one can? 36 levels from warm-up to expert, each with its fewest-moves target proved by computer, with undo, restart and hints from a real solver.
Molecule Lab 14+ ~15m Covalent bonding as taught at GCSE: how many bonds each atom makes (H 1, Cl 1, O 2, N 3, C 4), single, double and triple bonds, and turning a formula into a structure, with isomers told apart (ethanol and methoxymethane are both C₂H₆O). Each finished molecule relaxes into its VSEPR shape and names its bond angle, so shapes of molecules come for free.
Logic Gates 14+ ~15m Boolean logic for GCSE Computer Science: reading truth tables, what AND, OR, NOT, XOR, NAND and NOR do, De Morgan’s laws, building any gate from NAND, and real circuits such as half and full adders, a multiplexer and a comparator. Every level is checked solvable by computer, with undo, solver hints and a gate guide.
